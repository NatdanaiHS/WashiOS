\begin{abstract}
Small-satellite flight software relies on interacting safety mechanisms -- poll/deadline supervision of a payload link, a task-health-gated hardware watchdog and a CRC-validated A/B bootloader -- that are critical for recovery but hard to verify as a whole. Bench evaluation of such mechanisms reports host-observed latencies and cannot establish worst-case bounds or reach states that a test campaign never exercised, and the closest formal-verification work reviewed here addresses protocols and kernels; the composed mechanisms of one prototype, checked against its own hardware traces, are not reported in the cited papers. This work verifies an abstracted design model of the three interacting mechanisms of an STM32 flight-software prototype as UPPAAL timed automata, with constants copied from the C++ source, against safety, bounded-response and liveness properties in TCTL. Across fifteen model configurations and 254 formulas every verdict matches a pre-declared oracle: after confirmed fault onset the first timeout occurs within 605\,ms and OFFLINE within 1609\,ms, recovery completes within 506\,ms, a stalled critical task is detected within 952\,ms and forces a watchdog reset, and the boot policy never launches an unvalidated image; each bound is tight in the model. Model checking yields three design consequences and exposes one defect, confirmed and fixed in source: a bypassed unconfirmed-boot limit under which a Pending fallback image receives one attempt instead of four. We then evaluate the verified models against frozen, hash-verified hardware traces from 90 fault-injection trials and further datasets, re-anchored at the confirmation events where the model's clocks start, as non-refuting consistency evidence: 179 of the 180 recorded intervals lie inside the bounds, and the exception, 17\,ms over, is reported as unresolved. The guarantees concern the abstracted design under stated jitter and channel assumptions; the work does not verify the deployed C++ implementation, the RTOS or the HAL.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Resource-constrained flight computers for CubeSat-class missions rely on a small set of deterministic safety mechanisms: a polling supervisor that detects a silent, corrupt, or late payload; a task-health registry that gates refresh of an independent hardware watchdog; and a bootloader that validates an image before jumping to it and otherwise falls back to a second slot or a safe beacon loop. They are simple to state and easy to bench-test, yet a campaign only shows the paths it drove, and host timestamps cannot separate controller timing from serial and OS overhead. An earlier, unpublished hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) campaign on the same prototype, whose frozen traces we reuse in Section~\ref{sec:conf}, observed the predefined markers in 30/30 trials per fault mode and could not claim controller-internal timing for that reason.

This paper asks what the design itself guarantees. We formalize the three mechanisms of an STM32 flight-software prototype (name withheld for review; firmware not yet released) as UPPAAL timed automata~\cite{alur1994,larsen1997,behrmann2004} with constants copied from the source, verify properties with \texttt{verifyta}, and use the frozen HIL traces not as the result but as offline consistency evidence for the models.

The contributions are bounded as follows. (1)~Timed-automata models and verified properties for the payload-link supervisor, the task-health watchdog gate and the A/B boot policy of one prototype: worst-case detection, OFFLINE, recovery and stall-to-reset bounds, each shown attained, the delay-acceptance boundary, and ordering, safety, boundedness and progress properties (Table~\ref{tab:props}). (2)~One defect found by model checking and three design consequences made precise by the models, all confirmed in source (Section~\ref{sec:findings}). (3)~Model-to-hardware consistency evidence from offline replay of frozen, hash-verified traces against the model's alphabet and bounds, under an anchor aligned with the model's clocks and an uncertainty quantified from the evidence itself. The replay can refute agreement; it does not establish conformance in the testing sense.
\textbf{Assurance limits.} We do not claim verification of the C++ implementation, correctness of the RTOS or vendor HAL, robustness to environmental or radiation effects, or any reliability estimate (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Related Work}
Timed automata~\cite{alur1994} and UPPAAL~\cite{larsen1997,behrmann2004} are the standard formalism and tool; we position the paper along five layers of the verification stack, then against work on the same mechanisms.

\textbf{RTOS semantics.} Singh et al.\ apply UPPAAL model checking to conformance testing of an ARINC~653 RTOS~\cite{singh2021}; Lin and Wang model the FreeRTOS Cortex-M4 port, find errors by model checking and reproduce the assertion failures on an STM32F429 board~\cite{lin2024}. Both verify the kernel; we verify application-level supervision above an abstracted kernel, so scheduler-originated faults lie outside our results (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). UPPAAL has also been applied to satellite communication software~\cite{alencar2014}; link-supervision bounds are not reported in the cited paper.

\textbf{Compositional scaling.} State explosion is a recognized limit of real-time model checking, met with compositionality and abstraction~\cite{jensen2000}. Our state spaces stay small (at most 12\,547 states) because the three mechanisms are verified as three networks whose coupling (the link task is a monitored task; a stall leaves the retained fault the boot policy reads) enters each network as an input, so a further task or link enlarges one network without multiplying it by the others.

\textbf{Property engineering.} UPPAAL checks only non-nested reachability, safety and liveness, so richer real-time requirements need observer automata, which pattern catalogs now generate~\cite{vogel2023}. Our properties were written by hand from the research questions, encoding bounded response over the clocks of the scenario's driver template (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}), and were not cross-checked against the catalog.

\textbf{Model-to-code assurance.} Integrated formal verification of safety-critical software~\cite{ge2018}, verified code generation from timed automata with runtime monitors~\cite{cho2025}, and UPPAAL-TRON, which drives an implementation online and yields a conformance verdict on it~\cite{larsen2005}, all close the model--code gap more strongly than we do. We replay recorded, hash-verified traces against the model's alphabet and bounds without driving the implementation, so an accepted trace shows only that the run did not contradict the model. The choice is deliberate: the only free interface is the debug UART that already carries the console markers, and exposing internal events would perturb the poll timing under test.

\textbf{Hybrid V\&V.} Pontes et al.\ combine model checking with the CoFI conformance-and-fault-injection methodology for space embedded software~\cite{pontes2014}; fault injection~\cite{arlat1990,batista2019} is the empirical counterpart. Formal verification is one layer of that case here, and the reused HIL campaign serves only as data.

\textbf{The same mechanisms.} Pan et al.\ verify CAN arbitration and fault confinement in UPPAAL~\cite{pan2014} and Johansson et al.\ a heartbeat-based failure detector~\cite{johansson2020}; neither reports a poll/deadline supervisor with a consecutive-timeout threshold. Ahmed and Safar verify an AUTOSAR Watchdog Manager by symbolic execution~\cite{ahmed2018}; Cutler and Beningo report a nanosatellite watchdog with separate clock and power domains that recovers restart-curable faults in flight~\cite{cutler2022}; Asch et al.\ frame software-defined watchdogs as eager deadlines for delayed or missing signals~\cite{asch2025}. Those designs emphasize recovery, whereas the gate verified here is fail-safe (F2). Boot code has been verified for memory safety~\cite{cook2020} and secure-boot properties~\cite{vasudevan2024}; slot selection, unconfirmed-boot limits and fallback across resets are not reported in the cited papers.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{System under study}
\label{sec:system}
The controller is a NUCLEO-G431RB (STM32G431RB, 128\,KiB flash, 32\,KiB RAM) running a FreeRTOS application with static allocation and no heap. Every constant below is quoted from the source and traced to its line in the artifact; Table~\ref{tab:trace} shows representative rows, including the source anchor of finding F4.

\textbf{Payload-link supervisor.} A 32-byte CRC-32 framed poll is sent every $P=500$\,ms. A valid response must arrive within $D=100$\,ms of the send instant; the deadline and the next poll are both anchored at that instant. On a timeout the consecutive-timeout counter increments and the link enters OFFLINE when it reaches $N=3$. A valid response resets the counter, sets ONLINE, and counts a recovery if the link was OFFLINE. A CRC-corrupted frame increments a counter but leaves the outstanding poll pending; a valid frame that arrives when no poll is pending is a sequence reject. The task services the controller once per $L=1$\,ms loop, receiving frames before evaluating the deadline.

\textbf{Watchdog gate.} Two critical tasks check in periodically: the heartbeat every 500\,ms with a 1500\,ms deadline, and the payload-link task every 100\,ms with an 800\,ms deadline. A watchdog task wakes every 50\,ms and evaluates the registry once 100\,ms have elapsed since the previous evaluation, expiring an entry whose last check-in is older than its deadline, and the first expiry records a watchdog-timeout and a safe-fail event in retained memory. The independent watchdog (IWDG, prescaler 16, reload 3999, LSI $\approx$32\,kHz, nominal 2.0\,s) is refreshed only while all critical entries are healthy. On this target the safe-fail callback does not reset the processor; the IWDG does.

\textbf{A/B boot policy.} On reset the bootloader recovers a CRC-protected retained fault log and boot metadata, applies a confirmed-boot flag if set, refuses to boot if the active slot is Pending with a boot count above 3 or the fault log holds a check-in failure or watchdog timeout, validates the active slot's state, vector table and CRC-32, falls back to the other slot, and otherwise records a safe-fail and enters an LED beacon loop.

\subsection{Payload-link model}
\label{sec:models}
The network has three templates. \emph{Controller} carries clocks for time since the last poll and since the outstanding poll, and its transitions mirror \texttt{preparePoll}, \texttt{acceptResponse} and \texttt{service}. \emph{Payload} answers a poll after a serialization interval $[3,4]$\,ms, preceded by a configured delay $d$ in DELAYED mode, or not at all in SILENT mode, or with a corrupted CRC in BAD\_CRC mode. \emph{Host} reproduces the frozen trial protocol: once the link is ONLINE it injects one fault mode, holds it for the campaign's 4\,s exposure, restores NORMAL, and observes for 3\,s; its transitions are the confirmation events at which the comparison anchors start (Section~\ref{sec:confmethod}). A response arriving with no poll pending is a sequence reject.

Abstractions: RTOS wakeup and ISR latency are bounded by a 2\,ms jitter constant (assumed, not measured); the channel is lossless except for the injected corruption; $d<P$ so responses never overlap the next poll. Which results rest on the jitter abstraction is explicit: Q1, Q7, Q8, W1, W2 and every boot property are stated without $J$, and each bounded-response and boundary bound is a closed form in $J$ (Table~\ref{tab:props}), so a larger measured jitter moves a bound without invalidating the proof. No value of $J$ covers a scheduler-induced delay beyond it, such as blocking or priority inversion; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} states why the code excludes it.



\subsection{Watchdog-gate model}
Two \emph{Task} instances check in with their periods and may be stalled by an \emph{Env} injector; a \emph{Supervisor} evaluates every 100\,ms with the source's strict-greater expiry test and refreshes the \emph{Iwdg} only when both are healthy; the IWDG resets when its clock reaches a value in $[1882,2170]$\,ms, derived from the datasheet LSI range of 29.5--34\,kHz over the full supply and temperature range~\cite{st2021ds12589}. Scenario~0 never stalls; Scenario~1 stalls one task after warm-up.





\subsection{Boot-policy model}
An untimed network (Fig.~\ref{fig:boot}): \emph{Boot} runs the policy over the retained variables (active slot, slot states, boot count, confirmed flag, retained critical fault, metadata corruption); \emph{App} may stall, setting the retained fault and resetting, reset cleanly, or, in a design variant, set the confirmed flag; \emph{Flash} corrupts an image, stages a Pending image in slot~B, or cold-starts the board, scrubbing retained SRAM. The shipped variant gives the application no confirm capability because no code under the application tree writes boot metadata.

\subsection{Consistency-evidence method}
\label{sec:confmethod}
The frozen traces were acquired with a confirmation-gated protocol: a host injects a payload fault mode over the payload board's debug UART, counts the trial only after the payload confirms the mode, observes the controller console for 4\,s, restores NORMAL, counts recovery only after the payload confirms restoration, and observes for 3\,s; both serial channels are logged byte-exact with host timestamps and hash-inventoried. 

The campaign reports each interval from the command send on a monotonic clock, but the model's clocks start at the confirmed mode change and the confirmed restoration, so the command anchor is structurally earlier and overstates the model-comparable interval. We therefore recompute every interval from the retained wall-clock stamp of the confirming serial event to that of the observed marker. Three uncertainties remain. Every command-anchored interval carries both a wall-clock and a monotonic stamp, and their disagreement bounds the wall-clock granularity at 15.4\,ms, with a 4.4\,ms median over the 180 intervals. Cross-channel read latency between the payload and controller serial channels is not separately measured. The clock-rate difference is systematic and the evidence measures it: the model's constants are in MCU time and the intervals in host time, and each nominal window carries the controller's poll counter beside host timestamps, and over 1200 consecutive polls the mean period is 498.83\,ms against a nominal 500\,ms, the two 65\,s windows agreeing to within 0.03\,\%, so the MCU base runs 0.235\,\% fast and host intervals are shorter by that factor, 1.4\,ms at the first-timeout bound; the mean falling \emph{below} nominal also shows that the per-poll overhead bounded by the 2\,ms jitter constant is not consumed on average. We therefore declare a falsification floor of 50\,ms, more than three times the demonstrated clock disagreement: the replay can refute a bound violation larger than the floor, and reports any smaller excess as unresolved instead of absorbing it.

Every model satisfies \texttt{A[] not deadlock}, though in the boot network that is a modeling sanity condition, since its environment templates always offer an enabled edge; progress is therefore stated explicitly: every outstanding poll is resolved and the controller polls again (Q11), a stall leads to a reset (W5), and every reset ends in a launch or the safe loop (B9). The bounds are inclusive and tight: as a strict inequality each formula holds and with $\ge$ each fails, so the event can occur exactly at the bound. They exceed a naive hand derivation by one jitter term per poll period, because the controller anchors the next poll at the actual send instant so wake-up jitter accumulates; the model caught that omission in our own first estimate.

\textbf{Sensitivity to the jitter bound.} The first-timeout bound is $601+2J$\,ms; the delay-acceptance boundary is more informative: the accepted region $d+T_{tx}^{\max}<D$ does not involve $J$, so its edge stays at 95\,ms, whereas the timed-out region $d+T_{tx}^{\min}>D{+}L{+}J$ moves with $J$, leaving an undetermined band of 96 to $98{+}J$\,ms. Re-verifying at $J \in \{0,1,5\}$\,ms confirms this: for $J \le 1$\,ms the model decides $d = 100$\,ms as a timeout, as the hardware did; from $J = 2$\,ms it is undecidable. The band is therefore a consequence of a conservative jitter bound rather than a limit of the model, and measuring $J$ would remove it.


\subsection{Design findings}
\label{sec:findings}
Model checking produced one defect (F4) and made three design consequences precise (F1--F3). For F1 and F2 the models quantify behavior that source inspection had already identified; only F4 was discovered by the checker.

\textbf{F1: the boot-confirmation path is unreachable in flight.} B7 holds in the shipped configuration and fails in the design variant. Inspection established the source fact first -- no file under the application tree references the boot metadata or the confirmed flag -- and it was then encoded as the shipped configuration's zero confirm capability, so the model contributes the consequence, not the discovery: a slot provisioned as Pending can leave that state only through the bootloader's attempt limit (B3, B8), never through confirmation. The prototype is safe because slot~A is provisioned as Confirmed at build time, not because confirmation is exercised.

\textbf{F2: critical stalls are fail-safe, not fail-operational.} W2 and W6 show that once a critical task expires the IWDG is never refreshed again and a reset follows within one IWDG period. B6 then shows the bootloader, seeing the retained watchdog-timeout event, enters the safe loop instead of relaunching until a cold start -- a composed behavior the component tests did not make visible.

\textbf{F3: the effective delay boundary is below the nominal deadline.} Q9 proves, in the model, acceptance whenever $d+T_{tx}^{\max}<D$ ($d\le95$\,ms) and a timeout with sequence-rejected late frames whenever $d+T_{tx}^{\min}>D+L+J$ ($d\ge101$\,ms); at $d=100$\,ms both outcomes are reachable (Q9c), because the earliest response and the latest deadline check coincide at 103\,ms under the 2\,ms jitter assumption. The frozen sweep (Section~\ref{sec:conf}) observed 90\,ms accepted and 100\,ms rejected in every valid trial on three board pairs, consistent with the model but not proven by it.

\textbf{F4: the fallback path bypasses the unconfirmed-boot limit.} B8 fails in both boot configurations and B8b shows the violation exists only on the fallback path. The limit check reads the state and boot count of the \emph{active} slot; when that slot fails validation and the other slot is Pending (a staged, unconfirmed update), the fallback launch inherits the boot count accumulated while the confirmed slot was running. B8c then shows that the next warm boot is refused. The counts make the defect exact. Every launch of either slot increments the count, only confirmation resets it, and the limit refuses a Pending active slot whose count exceeds 3, so a freshly staged Pending slot is launched at counts 0--3 and refused at 4: four launches (B3). After $k$ warm resets of a Confirmed slot the count is $k$ and unchecked; when that slot then fails validation, the fallback launches the Pending slot at count $k$ without a check, so it is refused after $4-k$ launches -- after one once $k\ge3$, the case of the regression test (launch at 3, count 4, next boot refused). A Pending fallback image therefore gets one attempt instead of four. The fix -- reset the attempt budget when the active slot changes -- was re-verified: B8 holds with every other boot property unchanged and B8c becomes vacuous; a native regression test reproduces the inherited count and fails without the fix.



\subsection{Consistency with frozen hardware traces}
\label{sec:conf}
The replay accepts every frozen dataset. In all 90 primary trials the predefined detector marker is the model's first observable event for that mode, and no trial recorded a controller restart marker. Both 65\,s nominal windows and the separate 605\,s observation show zero counter deltas, as Q1 requires. In the delay sweep the 6 rows at 50 and 90\,ms were accepted and the 9 rows at 110, 150 and 250\,ms timed out, as Q9a and Q9b predict, while the 3 rows at 100\,ms fall inside the undetermined band; the two F411 pairs add 18 consistent rows at 90 and 110\,ms and in the nominal condition, and 6 rows at 100\,ms, likewise undetermined. The 2 BAD\_CRC mechanism traces show the CRC reject preceding the first timeout, as Q7 requires.

Table~\ref{tab:desc} compares the recorded intervals with the verified bounds under both anchors. Under the confirmation anchor 179 of the 180 intervals lie within the bounds. The exception is one BAD\_CRC recovery interval of 523\,ms against the 506\,ms bound, an excess of 17\,ms (18\,ms after the clock-rate correction), larger than the 15.4\,ms clock disagreement the evidence demonstrates, so timestamp granularity does not fully account for it; the remainder lies within the unmeasured cross-channel read latency, and the interval is reported as unresolved rather than as consistent or violating. No interval exceeds a bound by more than the 50\,ms falsification floor, so the replay finds no refutation of the model's bounds at the resolution the recorded evidence supports.

At the campaign's own anchor four of the six maxima exceed the bounds by 5 to 25\,ms, so the same traces would appear to violate bounds they in fact respect.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Threats to validity and limitations}
The models are hand-derived. Deadlock freedom and progress are properties of the models, not of the firmware's task interactions; task-level blocking is excluded by construction, since the flight code uses no semaphores, queues, mutexes, event groups or task notifications and blocks only in delays, non-blocking critical sections and UART calls with a 10\,ms timeout -- verified by source inspection, not by the models. The transition-to-source trace and the offline replay narrow the gap but do not prove refinement; a code-level check of the header-only controller with a bounded model checker is future work. The jitter bound's worst case is not measured; serialization is derived, not measured; the channel is assumed lossless apart from the injected corruption; and the IWDG bracket comes from the datasheet LSI specification~\cite{st2021ds12589}. The bounds are parametric rather than platform-specific: Table~\ref{tab:props} states each as a closed form in $P$, $D$, $L$, $N$, $J$ and $T_{tx}$, of which only $J$ and $T_{tx}$ depend on the MCU and RTOS. Another target therefore needs those two constants re-measured and the same queries re-run, not new models. The consistency data come from one primary board pair and two further pairs of a different MCU family under one fixed configuration, and say nothing about device populations. Nothing here addresses environmental, electrical, or radiation-induced faults, and nothing constitutes qualification evidence. The empirical support is uneven (Table~\ref{tab:scope}): only the link bounds are replayed against traces; the watchdog and boot results rest on model checking, source inspection and native tests, and injecting stalls and boot faults is future work.

\textbf{Property expressiveness.} UPPAAL checks a restricted TCTL fragment -- invariants, reachability and leads-to, without nesting. Of the 44 formulas, 34 are invariants, 4 reachability and 6 leads-to. Bounded response is therefore an invariant over a clock of the driver template that starts at the confirmation event, so the driver plays the observer role a pattern catalog would generate~\cite{vogel2023}; ordering properties use Boolean flags set on the controller's transitions. No alternative encoding was cross-checked, and a mis-stated phase predicate would pass both tightness twins. As inequalities over console-observable events the bounds could also run as monitors on the debug UART, as model-paired runtime monitoring does~\cite{zilio2023,cho2025}; we have not done so.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
The artifact (models and queries, batch script with retained \texttt{verifyta} outputs and SHA-256 inventory, verdict oracle and checker, line-numbered model-to-source trace, trace replay with unit tests and JSON report, frozen HIL datasets with inventories) is released on acceptance; the firmware is not, so the trace is pinned to a tagged internal revision and source excerpts can be supplied through the chairs.

\section{Conclusion}
Model checking the composed supervisor, watchdog gate and boot policy of an STM32 flight-software prototype yields worst-case bounds a bench cannot provide and exposes one defect in shipped safety logic with three design consequences. All 254 verdicts across fifteen configurations equal a pre-declared oracle, the defect is fixed and the fix re-verified, and replaying frozen hardware traces refutes none of the bounds at the resolution the recorded evidence supports. It shows non-refuting trace consistency with hardware behavior and does not verify the deployed C++ implementation, the RTOS or the HAL.
