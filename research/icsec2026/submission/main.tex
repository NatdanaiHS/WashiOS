\title{Confirmation-Gated HIL Fault-Injection Trials for Auditable Payload-Link Supervision}
\author{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
An injection request does not show that the injected endpoint entered the configured fault mode. This distinction matters when controller detection and recovery are scored from separate observations. We present a confirmation-gated hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) trial protocol for a supervised payload link. Payload-side confirmation of configured fault-mode entry gates detection scoring, and payload-side confirmation of NORMAL restoration gates recovery scoring. These two gates are operationalized as distinct requested, activated, detected, restored, and recovered events. Byte-preserving controller and payload traces and hash-verified summaries support auditability. In a setup-bounded demonstration, one G431/G474 pair completed 90 sequential trials, 30 each for silence, bad CRC, and delay. All 90 trials had confirmed activation and restoration; the predefined detector and recovery markers appeared in 30/30 valid trials per mode. Host-observed median command-to-marker intervals ranged from 359.5 to 406.0~ms for detection and from 296.5 to 359.0~ms for recovery. Separate evidence comprises a 605-s nominal observation, two BAD\_CRC mechanism observations, and two 12-row F411 pair campaigns reported with separate denominators. The results demonstrate the protocol on the evaluated setups. They are not reliability, device-population, MCU-equivalence, or controller-internal timing estimates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Embedded controllers often supervise whether a peripheral responds, whether a received frame is valid, and whether an unavailable peer returns to service. HIL fault injection can exercise these paths, but its evidence is ambiguous if a sent injector command is treated as proof that the endpoint entered the configured fault mode. The same problem recurs at recovery: requesting restoration does not show that the endpoint returned to NORMAL mode. Controller markers should be scored only after those endpoint mode transitions are separately confirmed.

Fault-injection methodology distinguishes faults, functional activation, readouts, and measures~\cite{arlat1990}. Mart\'{i}nez \emph{et al.} confirm effective pin-level injection and filter invalid event sequences~\cite{martinez1999}. Automotive HIL and nanosatellite studies provide programmatic faults, communication-bus interception, multi-point observation, logging, and test traceability~\cite{abboush2022,abboush2024,batista2019,conceicao2025}. The gap addressed here is therefore not activation verification itself. Rather, in the closest studies considered here, the cited methods do not specify a stage-specific scoring rule that conditions detection on separate confirmation that the endpoint entered the configured fault mode and recovery on separate confirmation that the endpoint returned to NORMAL mode.

We therefore propose a two-stage confirmation-gated experimental protocol. A command is a request, not confirmation of an endpoint mode transition. Separate payload-side mode confirmation establishes configured fault-mode entry or restoration to NORMAL; only then can a controller-side detector or recovery marker be scored. The five event classes operationalize these gates. Host timestamps, exact received bytes, readable renderings, and hash-verified frozen summaries support auditability.

The paper's one central methodological contribution is the two-stage confirmation-gated scoring rule: payload-side confirmation of configured fault-mode entry makes detection eligible, and payload-side NORMAL confirmation makes recovery eligible. The distinct requested, activated, detected, restored, and recovered events operationalize this rule. The byte-preserving, hash-verified evidence workflow implements its audit support, and the setup-bounded campaigns evaluate its operation; neither is claimed as a separate research contribution.

The evaluated firmware and boards are context, not an architecture contribution or a new HIL framework, injector, simulator, or fault model. The study does not claim comparative advantage, reliability, population generality, flight qualification, mission assurance, MCU-family equivalence, or complete-system validation.

\section{Related Work}

Foundational fault-injection methodology distinguishes simulation- and prototype-based evaluation, including hardware- and software-implemented techniques, and couples controlled injection with monitoring of system effects~\cite{hsueh1997}. Work on fault-injection validity further separates requested faults from realized effects. Arlat \emph{et al.} define fault, functional-activation, readout, and measure sets (FARM)~\cite{arlat1990}. Their activation set functionally exercises the system; separate endpoint confirmation that a requested condition became active is not reported in the cited publication. Mart\'{i}nez \emph{et al.} use AFIT for high-speed pin-level injection and distinguish Activated Injection from Effective Error; an injector circuit asserts the latter only when the selected FTS pin is forced~\cite{martinez1999}. They discard non-effective injections, glitches, and invalid event sequences before analysis and measure detection latency from Effective Error. A separately observed restoration-to-normal gate before recovery scoring is not reported in the cited paper. Van der Kouwe \emph{et al.} distinguish inserted faults as an input fault load from the subset activated during execution as the output fault load when analyzing software-injection distortion~\cite{vanderkouwe2014}. Payload-endpoint confirmation and restoration gates are not applicable to that study design.

Automated HIL frameworks address configurable fault campaigns and system-level observation. Short \emph{et al.} combine statistical fault-injection testing with online model-based performance monitoring in an automotive HIL methodology and compare eight embedded-control designs in a case study~\cite{short2008}. Abboush \emph{et al.} configure fault type, location, time, and duration in real-time automotive HIL experiments and record multiple system signals for evaluation~\cite{abboush2022}. Their later framework automates campaigns across virtual driving scenarios, including multiple fault locations, and reports fault deactivation followed by return toward a safe state~\cite{abboush2024}. Separate injected-endpoint confirmation before detection scoring and separate restoration confirmation before recovery scoring are not reported in the cited Abboush publications.

Communication-level and nanosatellite work addresses physical injection, bus interception, and multi-point observation. Blanc \emph{et al.} apply pin-level physical injection to TTP/C communication controllers, including faults induced directly on communication lines and data errors at the controller-host interface~\cite{blanc2009}. Batista \emph{et al.} present FEM as a scripted interceptor for MIL/HIL nanosatellite robustness testing~\cite{batista2019}. FEM observes both low interfaces, returns exchanged-message data to its controller, and reports transmitted and received values and delays in MIL test tables. Only a small HIL set was attempted before testing was suspended after interface mismatches were found. Interface observation is reported, but per-trial confirmation gates for detector or recovery score eligibility are not reported in the cited paper. Concei\c{c}\~ao and Mattiello-Francisco organize interoperability testing into six MIL, SIL, and HIL modules, reuse model-derived tests, and use FEM for bus-fault tests~\cite{conceicao2025}.

Together, these studies establish activation or effective-injection confirmation, invalid-sequence filtering, communication-bus injection, multi-point observation, and test traceability as prior art. The central distinction here is only the combined two-stage scoring rule: separate payload-side confirmation of configured fault-mode entry gates detection, and separate payload-side NORMAL confirmation gates recovery. The five retained events operationalize those gates, while exact-byte traces and hashes support auditability. The protocol complements rather than replaces AFIT-style filtering, general HIL architectures, FEM, or SATS. ``Not reported in the cited paper'' is limited to that publication and does not imply that a system lacks the feature.

\section{System Context and Protocol}

\subsection{Evaluated Link}

The primary setup connects a NUCLEO-G431RB controller to a NUCLEO-G474RE payload simulator through a 115200-baud, 8N1 UART with common ground. The controller polls the payload and exposes timeout, rejection, OFFLINE, and recovery markers. The commandable payload returns NORMAL responses, withholds responses in SILENT mode, corrupts response CRCs in BAD\_CRC mode, and delays responses by 250~ms in DELAYED mode. The inspected controller deadline is 100~ms.

As Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol} shows, the host sends fault-control commands and separately captures controller (OBS\_C) and payload (OBS\_P) serial streams. These are separate observation channels collected on the same host; no stronger host or statistical independence is implied. The topology is the minimum context needed to define the protocol.

\subsection{Validity and Scoring}

For trial $i$, let $A_i,S_i,E_i\in\{0,1\}$ indicate, respectively, payload confirmation that the endpoint entered the configured fault mode, payload confirmation that it returned to NORMAL, and the presence of a harness invalid reason. Define the complete-trial validity indicator as
\begin{equation}
V_i = A_i \land S_i \land \neg E_i.
\end{equation}
Detection eligibility remains stage-specific: without $A_i$, the trial enters neither the detection nor recovery denominator. If $A_i=1$ but $S_i=0$, detection remains scoreable while recovery is unscored. This stage-specific rule does not change the primary results because all 90 primary trials contained both confirmations.

The payload firmware assigns the selected mode and configured delay, when applicable, before emitting the matching mode-confirmation line. That confirmation therefore establishes the scorer's causal eligibility boundary but is not an independent physical measurement of UART behavior. Because OBS\_C and OBS\_P are separate host-captured serial channels without a verified total order, a controller marker recorded before matching activation confirmation makes the cross-channel detection ordering temporally ambiguous. Detection is then left unscored rather than classified as DETECTED or NOT\_DETECTED; this ordering ambiguity does not by itself invalidate the trial or alter independently eligible recovery scoring.

To exercise the stage-specific branches without changing the HIL records, we derived five explicitly synthetic event-sequence mutations from valid BAD\_CRC trial R005\_P02 and replayed them through a separate scorer. Missing or wrong-mode activation confirmation left detection and recovery unscored; missing restoration confirmation after valid activation left detection scored and recovery unscored. A controller marker assigned a host timestamp before matching payload confirmation produced temporally ambiguous cross-channel ordering and left detection unscored, while recovery remained independently scoreable; a duplicate matching activation confirmation was flagged and counted once. The mutation manifest, expected and actual outputs, assertions, and source and derived SHA-256 records are retained separately, and all checked source hashes remained unchanged. These cases check scorer control flow only; they are not HIL trials and are excluded from the 90-trial and all other empirical denominators.

The executed sequence comprised establishing and confirming a NORMAL precondition, waiting a seeded offset, requesting a fault mode, requiring the matching payload confirmation, observing the controller for 4~s, requesting NORMAL, requiring payload confirmation of NORMAL, and observing recovery for 3~s. Seed 20260830 fixed the order and offsets of 90 sequential primary trials, 30 per mode.

Mode-specific detector predicates were fixed before analysis. SILENT and DELAYED use the first post-request timeout or OFFLINE marker. BAD\_CRC uses the first explicit CRC-rejection marker; OFFLINE is secondary. Recovery requires a recovery marker after the NORMAL request and confirmed restoration. A restart field records only whether a post-injection link-start marker appears; absence of that marker is not proof that no reset occurred.

At the evaluated controller revision, a CRC-invalid response increments the CRC-rejection counter and returns without clearing the outstanding request. The 100-ms deadline can then expire and increment a consecutive-timeout count; the third consecutive timeout sets OFFLINE. A later accepted response clears the request and timeout streak, sets ONLINE, and records recovery if the prior state was OFFLINE. These source-level statements explain marker order but do not measure MCU execution time.

\subsection{Observation and Audit Model}

Each raw serial record contains host UTC time, exact received bytes, and an escaped readable rendering. Controller and payload files remain separate. During acquisition, the harness computes detection latency from host-monotonic times for the fault-command send and the first predefined controller marker. Recovery latency is computed analogously from the NORMAL-command send and the recovery marker and is populated only with restoration confirmation. The result rows retain these computed intervals, while raw logs retain UTC and exact bytes. Both intervals include UART, USB, driver, scheduling, and host timestamp effects; host and MCU clocks were not synchronized.

Machine validation cross-checked 90 plan rows, 90 result rows, and 180 controller/payload raw logs. Frozen JSON summaries and validations are identified by SHA-256. Table-generation scripts verify input hashes before deterministic projection. Analysis is descriptive: counts, observed proportions, median, inclusive-linear quartiles, IQR, minimum, and maximum. No hypothesis test, confidence claim, or between-mode ranking is used.

\section{Discussion and Validity Boundaries}

Two-stage confirmation gating changes what a scored result means. A detector count is conditional on payload confirmation of configured fault-mode entry, and a recovery count is conditional on payload confirmation of return to NORMAL; neither confirmation is an independent measurement of the corresponding UART behavior on the wire. Missing activation confirmation excludes the trial from both detection and recovery scoring. With confirmed activation but missing restoration, detection remains scoreable while recovery is invalid and unscored. The five-event record makes these eligibility boundaries explicit; exact-byte traces and hashes support their audit. The primary campaign demonstrates that the complete requested-to-recovered chain can be operated and audited on the evaluated G431/G474 setup. It demonstrates protocol operability and auditability, but does not estimate how often request-only scoring would produce a different result because no ungated comparative baseline was run. The N0 windows are setup-integrity observations, not interleaved no-fault treatment controls; they therefore neither establish a comparative fault effect nor quantify natural system variance. The evaluation instead asks whether predefined controller outcomes occur after separately confirmed endpoint conditions; comparative causal attribution would require an interleaved control design beyond the present protocol evaluation. The separate F411 campaigns demonstrate the same confirmation rules on two further pair-specific setups. Neither result estimates how often unobserved devices would behave similarly.

Construct validity is bounded by observable endpoint behavior and predefined log markers. SILENT, BAD\_CRC, and DELAYED do not represent all communication faults and are not equivalent to electrical, environmental, or radiation-induced faults. Markers operationalize detection and recovery but do not expose every internal state. Literal heartbeat or watchdog strings are not treated as independent health measurements.

Internal validity benefits from a confirmed NORMAL precondition, seeded order and offsets, fixed observation windows, separate endpoint logs, and stage-specific eligibility rules for missing confirmations. Sequential trials still share hardware, host, thermal, state-history, and timing conditions. The two 65-s N0 windows, separate 605-s observation, and one 65-s gate per F411 pair are duration bounded.

External and timing validity are similarly narrow. One primary pair and two purposively evaluated F411 pairs do not define a device population. Other delay values, corruption patterns, intermittent behavior, concurrent loads, and recovery horizons remain untested. Host serial timestamps include transport and scheduling effects. Temperature, vibration, electromagnetic conditions, power transients, and radiation were not varied or measured. The evidence supports no environmental robustness, flight-qualification, mission-assurance, or complete-system claim.

\balance
\section{Reproducibility}

The retained primary package contains the fixed seed and run plan, per-trial rows, 180 byte-preserving raw logs, validation output, summaries, firmware provenance, and SHA-256 inventories. Separate frozen paths retain the 605-s observation, both BAD\_CRC traces, and each F411 campaign. The manuscript references these existing records; it does not copy empirical evidence. Hash-checking generators project table inputs deterministically and reject a changed source. This workflow makes the path from endpoint bytes to reported values inspectable without treating a hash as evidence that the experimental construct is externally valid.

\section{Conclusion}

This paper presented a two-stage confirmation-gated HIL trial protocol for payload-link supervision. A requested injection becomes scoreable only after separate payload-side confirmation of configured fault-mode entry; recovery becomes scoreable only after separate payload-side NORMAL confirmation. These confirmations establish scoring boundaries but do not independently measure corresponding UART behavior on the wire. Five distinct event classes operationalize those gates, and byte-preserving endpoint traces with hash-verified summaries support auditability. The primary 90-trial campaign, separate nominal and BAD\_CRC observations, and two pair-separated F411 campaigns demonstrate the protocol only on the evaluated setups. The result is methodological and setup bounded, with no reliability, population, MCU-equivalence, or internal-timing inference.
