\begin{abstract}
Small-satellite flight software relies on interacting safety mechanisms -- poll/deadline supervision of a payload link, a task-health-gated hardware watchdog and a CRC-validated A/B bootloader -- whose worst-case behavior bench tests cannot bound; the composed mechanisms of one prototype, checked against its own hardware traces, are not reported in the cited formal-verification papers. This work verifies an abstracted design model of the three mechanisms of an STM32 flight-software prototype as UPPAAL timed automata, with constants copied from the C++ source and a scheduling slip measured from the prototype's own logs, against safety, bounded-response and liveness properties in TCTL. Across twenty-one model configurations and 368 formulas every verdict matches a pre-declared oracle: after confirmed fault onset the first timeout occurs within 603\,ms and OFFLINE within 1607\,ms, recovery completes within 516\,ms (663, 1667 and 576\,ms when a periodic status report falls in the window), a stalled critical task is detected within 952\,ms and forces a watchdog reset, and the boot policy never launches an unvalidated image. Model checking yields three design consequences and one defect, fixed in source: a bypassed unconfirmed-boot limit that leaves a Pending fallback image one attempt instead of four. The real C++ of all three mechanisms, executed on the host under the model's assumptions, stays within every bound and exhibits the defect before the fix. Frozen hardware traces from 90 fault-injection trials and further datasets, re-anchored where the model's clocks start, give non-refuting consistency evidence: all 180 recorded intervals lie inside the bounds. The guarantees concern the abstracted design under stated assumptions, not the deployed C++, the RTOS or the HAL.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Resource-constrained flight computers for CubeSat-class missions rely on a small set of deterministic safety mechanisms: a polling supervisor that detects a silent, corrupt, or late payload; a task-health registry that gates refresh of an independent hardware watchdog; and a bootloader that validates an image before jumping to it and otherwise falls back to a second slot or a safe beacon loop. They are simple to state and easy to bench-test, yet a campaign only shows the paths it drove, and host timestamps cannot separate controller timing from serial and OS overhead. An earlier, unpublished hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) campaign on the same prototype, whose frozen traces we reuse in Section~\ref{sec:conf}, observed the predefined markers in 30/30 trials per fault mode and could not claim controller-internal timing for that reason.

This paper asks what the design itself guarantees. We formalize the three mechanisms of an STM32 flight-software prototype (\ifanon name withheld for review\else WashiOS\fi; firmware not yet released) as UPPAAL timed automata~\cite{alur1994,larsen1997,behrmann2004} with constants copied from the source, verify properties with \texttt{verifyta}, execute the real C++ against the same properties, and use the frozen HIL traces not as the result but as offline consistency evidence for the models.

Contributions: (1)~timed-automata models of the three mechanisms with verified worst-case detection, OFFLINE, recovery and stall-to-reset bounds, each shown attained, the delay-acceptance boundary, and safety and progress properties (Table~\ref{tab:props}); (2)~one defect found by model checking and three design consequences, all confirmed in source (Section~\ref{sec:findings}); (3)~code-level evidence that the real C++ stays within the bounds (Section~\ref{sec:code}); (4)~consistency evidence from offline replay of frozen, hash-verified traces under an anchor aligned with the model's clocks, which can refute agreement but does not establish conformance in the testing sense.
\textbf{Assurance limits.} We do not claim verification of the C++ implementation, correctness of the RTOS or vendor HAL, robustness to environmental or radiation effects, or any reliability estimate (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Related Work}
Timed automata~\cite{alur1994} and UPPAAL~\cite{larsen1997,behrmann2004} are the standard formalism and tool.
\textbf{RTOS semantics.} UPPAAL has been applied to conformance testing of an ARINC~653 RTOS~\cite{singh2021}, and model checking of the FreeRTOS Cortex-M4 port found errors reproduced on an STM32F429 board~\cite{lin2024}; both verify the kernel, whereas we verify application-level supervision above an abstracted kernel. UPPAAL has also been applied to satellite communication software~\cite{alencar2014}; link-supervision bounds are not reported in the cited paper.
\textbf{Scaling and properties.} State explosion is met with compositionality and abstraction~\cite{jensen2000}; our three networks stay small (at most 55\,462 states) because their coupling enters each as an input. Pattern catalogs generate observer automata for requirements beyond UPPAAL's fragment~\cite{vogel2023}; our properties were written by hand and not cross-checked against one.
\textbf{Model-to-code assurance.} Integrated formal verification~\cite{ge2018}, verified code generation from timed automata with runtime monitors~\cite{cho2025} and UPPAAL-TRON online testing~\cite{larsen2005} close the model--code gap more strongly than our replay of recorded traces, which shows only that a run did not contradict the model; the only free interface, the debug UART, already carries the console markers. Model checking has been combined with conformance testing and fault injection for space software~\cite{pontes2014}; fault injection~\cite{arlat1990,batista2019} is the empirical counterpart.
\textbf{The same mechanisms.} CAN arbitration and fault confinement~\cite{pan2014} and a heartbeat failure detector~\cite{johansson2020} have been verified in UPPAAL, neither with a poll/deadline supervisor and a consecutive-timeout threshold; an AUTOSAR Watchdog Manager has been verified by symbolic execution~\cite{ahmed2018}; a nanosatellite watchdog recovers restart-curable faults in flight~\cite{cutler2022}, and software-defined watchdogs act as eager deadlines~\cite{asch2025}. Those designs emphasize recovery, whereas the gate verified here is fail-safe (F2). Boot code has been verified for memory safety~\cite{cook2020} and secure boot~\cite{vasudevan2024}; slot selection, unconfirmed-boot limits and fallback across resets are not reported in the cited papers.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{System under study}
\label{sec:system}
The controller is a NUCLEO-G431RB (STM32G431RB, 128\,KiB flash, 32\,KiB RAM) running a FreeRTOS application with static allocation and no heap. Every constant below is quoted from the source and traced to its line in the artifact; Table~\ref{tab:trace} shows representative rows, including the source anchor of finding F4.

\textbf{Payload-link supervisor.} A 32-byte CRC-32 framed poll is sent every $P=500$\,ms. A valid response must arrive within $D=100$\,ms of the send instant; the deadline and the next poll are both anchored at that instant. On a timeout the consecutive-timeout counter increments and the link enters OFFLINE when it reaches $N=3$. A valid response resets the counter, sets ONLINE, and counts a recovery if the link was OFFLINE. A CRC-corrupted frame increments a counter but leaves the outstanding poll pending; a valid frame that arrives when no poll is pending is a sequence reject. The task services the controller once per $L=1$\,ms loop, receiving frames before evaluating the deadline.

\textbf{Watchdog gate.} Two critical tasks check in periodically: the heartbeat every 500\,ms with a 1500\,ms deadline, and the payload-link task every 100\,ms with an 800\,ms deadline. A watchdog task wakes every 50\,ms and evaluates the registry once 100\,ms have elapsed since the previous evaluation, expiring an entry whose last check-in is older than its deadline, and the first expiry records a watchdog-timeout and a safe-fail event in retained memory. The independent watchdog (IWDG), a hardware timer clocked by the internal low-speed oscillator (LSI, $\approx$32\,kHz) that resets the MCU unless refreshed, has a nominal 2.0\,s period (prescaler 16, reload 3999) and is refreshed only while all critical entries are healthy. On this target the safe-fail callback does not reset the processor; the IWDG does.

\textbf{A/B boot policy.} Each of two flash slots has a state: Empty, Valid, Pending (a staged image the application has not yet confirmed), Confirmed or Bad. On reset the bootloader recovers a CRC-protected retained fault log and boot metadata, applies a confirmed-boot flag if set, refuses to boot if the active slot is Pending with a boot count above 3 or the fault log holds a check-in failure or watchdog timeout, validates the active slot's state, vector table and CRC-32, falls back to the other slot, and otherwise records a safe-fail and enters an LED beacon loop.

\subsection{Payload-link model}
\label{sec:models}
The network has four templates. \emph{Controller} has clocks for the last poll, the outstanding poll and a received response; its transitions mirror \texttt{preparePoll}, \texttt{acceptResponse} and \texttt{service}, and a received frame is consumed at the next loop pass, before the deadline is evaluated. \emph{Payload} reads its mode when it decodes the request and answers after the round trip below, preceded by a configured delay $d$ in DELAYED mode, or not at all in SILENT mode, or with a corrupted CRC in BAD\_CRC mode. \emph{Status} blocks the link task, which then neither polls, evaluates the deadline nor consumes a frame, for at most $S$ once per 5\,s. \emph{Host} reproduces the frozen trial protocol: once the link is ONLINE it injects one fault mode, holds it for the campaign's 4\,s exposure, restores NORMAL, and observes for 3\,s; its transitions are the confirmation events at which the comparison anchors start (Section~\ref{sec:confmethod}).

Abstractions: RTOS wake-up and ISR latency are bounded by $J{=}2$\,ms per wake-up (assumed). The round trip is derived from source: the request is decoded 2--9\,ms after the poll stamp (32-byte poll at 115200\,baud, the responder's 1\,ms loop, at most one blocking debug line), the response arrives 2--4\,ms later, and \texttt{HAL\_Delay}$(d)$ lasts $d$ to $d{+}1$\,ms. The status slip is measured: in the frozen 605\,s observation the poll schedule slips by 10 to 54.5\,ms (median 30.4\,ms, $n{=}241$) at every status report and by no more than host noise elsewhere, so the model blocks the link task for at most $S{=}60$\,ms per report; the slip is host-observed and its cause is not established. The channel is lossless except for the injected corruption; $d<P$ so responses never overlap the next poll. Q1, Q7, Q8, W1, W2 and every boot property are stated without $J$ or $S$, and each bounded-response and boundary bound is a closed form in them (Table~\ref{tab:props}), so a measured value moves a bound without invalidating the proof. No value of $J$ covers blocking or priority inversion, which the code excludes (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).



\subsection{Watchdog-gate model}
Two \emph{Task} instances check in with their periods and may be stalled by an \emph{Env} injector; a \emph{Supervisor} evaluates every 100\,ms with the source's strict-greater expiry test and refreshes the \emph{Iwdg} only when both are healthy; the IWDG resets when its clock reaches a value in $[1882,2170]$\,ms, derived from the datasheet LSI range of 29.5--34\,kHz over the full supply and temperature range~\cite{st2021ds12589}. Scenario~0 never stalls; Scenario~1 stalls one task after warm-up.





\subsection{Boot-policy model}
An untimed network (Fig.~\ref{fig:boot}): \emph{Boot} runs the policy over the retained variables (active slot, slot states, boot count, confirmed flag, retained critical fault, metadata corruption); \emph{App} may stall, setting the retained fault and resetting, reset cleanly, or, in a design variant, set the confirmed flag; \emph{Flash} corrupts an image, stages a Pending image in slot~B, or cold-starts the board, scrubbing retained SRAM. The shipped variant gives the application no confirm capability because no code under the application tree writes boot metadata.

\subsection{Consistency-evidence method}
\label{sec:confmethod}
The frozen traces come from a confirmation-gated protocol: a host injects a fault mode over the payload board's debug UART, counts the trial only after the payload confirms the mode, observes the controller console for 4\,s, restores NORMAL, waits for the payload's confirmation and observes for 3\,s; both serial channels are logged byte-exact with host timestamps and hash-inventoried. The campaign measured intervals from the command send, which precedes the model's clock start; we instead use the \emph{confirmation anchor}, the host stamp of the payload's own confirmation line, and measure to the stamp of the controller's marker. Three uncertainties remain. The campaign's command-anchored intervals were taken on a monotonic clock with a 15.6\,ms tick; their disagreement with the wall-clock stamps, at most 15.4\,ms, measures that tick, not the wall clock. The wall-clock stamps are finer: in the 605\,s observation each stamp deviates from the poll phase of its neighbors by at most 8.6\,ms (99th percentile 5.2\,ms), which bounds per-stamp read latency on the controller channel; cross-channel latency is not separately measured. The clock-rate difference is systematic: between status reports the median poll period is 495.9\,ms of host time, and since the controller never polls earlier than 500\,ms of MCU time, the MCU time base runs at least 0.84\,\% fast and host intervals are shorter by that factor, about 5\,ms at the first-timeout bound. We declare a \emph{falsification floor} of 50\,ms, more than five times the largest read residual: the replay can refute a bound violation larger than the floor and reports any smaller excess as unresolved.

Every model satisfies \texttt{A[] not deadlock}; progress is stated explicitly (Q11, W5, B9) because the boot environment always offers an enabled edge. Each bound holds strictly and fails with $\ge$, so it is attained; it exceeds a naive derivation by one jitter term per poll period because the next poll is anchored at the actual send instant, an omission the model caught in our first estimate. In the DELAYED 90 band the recovery bound holds but is not attained (worst case 513\,ms); our oracle had predicted otherwise and was corrected with the reason recorded. The first-timeout bound is $599+2J+S$\,ms; the acceptance edge $d\le 85$\,ms involves neither $J$ nor $S$, whereas the timeout edge $d\ge98+J+S$ moves with both, leaving a band of 86 to 99\,ms, or 86 to 159\,ms with a status report; at $J=5$\,ms, $d=100$\,ms becomes undetermined.

\subsection{Design findings}
\label{sec:findings}
Model checking produced one defect (F4) and made three design consequences precise (F1--F3). For F1 and F2 the models quantify behavior that source inspection had already identified; only F4 was discovered by the checker.

\textbf{F1: the boot-confirmation path is unreachable in flight.} B7 holds in the shipped configuration and fails in the design variant: no application code writes the boot metadata, so a Pending slot can leave that state only through the attempt limit (B3, B8). Inspection found this first; the model contributes the consequence. The prototype is safe because slot~A is provisioned as Confirmed at build time.

\textbf{F2: critical stalls are fail-safe, not fail-operational.} W2 and W6 show that once a critical task expires the IWDG is never refreshed again and a reset follows within one IWDG period. B6 then shows the bootloader, seeing the retained watchdog-timeout event, enters the safe loop until a cold start -- a composed behavior the component tests did not show.

\textbf{F3: the effective delay boundary is below the nominal deadline.} Q9 proves, in the model, acceptance for $d\le 85$\,ms and a timeout with sequence-rejected late frames for $d\ge100$\,ms ($d\ge160$\,ms when a status report delays the deadline check); in between both outcomes are reachable. The frozen sweep accepted 90\,ms and rejected 100\,ms in every valid trial on three board pairs, and the executed controller code times out at 90\,ms in 13 of 7\,500 simulated trials, so the band is a property of the code, not of the abstraction.

\textbf{F4: the fallback path bypasses the unconfirmed-boot limit.} B8 fails in both boot configurations, and B8b shows the violation exists only on the fallback path. The limit check reads the \emph{active} slot; when that slot fails validation and the other slot is Pending, the fallback launch inherits the boot count accumulated by the confirmed slot. Every launch increments the count and only confirmation resets it, so a freshly staged Pending slot is launched at counts 0--3 and refused at 4 (B3), whereas after $k$ warm resets of the confirmed slot the fallback launches it at count $k$ unchecked and it is refused after $4-k$ launches -- after one once $k\ge3$ (B8c). The fix resets the attempt budget when the active slot changes. It was re-verified (B8 holds, every other boot property unchanged), a native regression test fails without it, and the exhaustive run of the C++ policy (Section~\ref{sec:code}) finds the violation on the pre-fix code only, on the fallback path.

\subsection{Code-level conformance}
\label{sec:code}
We compiled the unmodified C++ of the three mechanisms on the host and executed it under the model's environment assumptions. For the payload link the controller, frame codec and command parser run in a 1\,\textmu s discrete-event simulation of the two task loops, including every blocking UART write; over 287\,500 randomized trials the largest first-timeout, OFFLINE and recovery intervals are 596.7, 1598.6 and 506.4\,ms without a status report and 633.1, 1640.1 and 529.8\,ms with one, all within the bounds, and the delay edges match Q9. For the watchdog gate the real registry, watchdog and runner, stalled at every 1\,ms phase, detect a link stall within 870.8\,ms and a heartbeat stall within 1586.9\,ms (W3) and never refresh after a failure (W2). For the boot policy an exhaustive search of every retained state reachable under the model's environment (324 to 528 states) confirms B1--B4, B6 and B9 and finds B8 violated on the pre-fix code only. The task loops are restated by hand and the payload runs are sampled, so this is conformance evidence, not a refinement proof.

\subsection{Consistency with frozen hardware traces}

The replay accepts every frozen dataset. In all 90 primary trials the predefined detector marker is the model's first observable event for that mode, and no trial recorded a controller restart marker. Both 65\,s nominal windows and the separate 605\,s observation show zero counter deltas, as Q1 requires. In the delay sweep the 3 rows at 50\,ms were accepted, as Q9a predicts, the 12 rows at 100 to 250\,ms timed out, as Q9b predicts, and the 3 rows at 90\,ms, accepted, fall inside the undetermined band; the two F411 pairs add 18 consistent rows at 100 and 110\,ms and in the nominal condition, and 6 rows at 90\,ms, likewise in the band. The 2 BAD\_CRC mechanism traces show the CRC reject preceding the first timeout, as Q7 requires.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Threats to validity and limitations}
The models are hand-derived, and deadlock freedom and progress are model properties. Task-level blocking is excluded by source inspection: the flight code uses no semaphores, queues, mutexes, event groups or task notifications. The trace, the code-level runs and the replay narrow the model--code gap without proving refinement. $J$ is assumed (host-observed periods between status reports spread over 491 to 500\,ms, consistent with it); $S$ is host-observed and its cause unknown; the round trip is derived; the channel is assumed lossless; the IWDG bracket comes from the datasheet~\cite{st2021ds12589}. Only $J$, the round trip and $S$ depend on the MCU, RTOS and console load, so another target needs those re-measured and the queries re-run, not new models. The traces come from three board pairs under one configuration and say nothing about device populations, environmental or radiation faults, or qualification. Only the link bounds are replayed against hardware traces (Table~\ref{tab:scope}); stall and boot-fault injection on hardware, with MCU-side timing of $J$ and $S$, is future work.

\textbf{Property expressiveness.} Of the 44 distinct formulas, 34 are invariants, 4 reachability and 6 leads-to. The scenario's driver template plays the observer role a pattern catalog would generate~\cite{vogel2023}, and ordering uses Boolean flags; no alternative encoding was cross-checked, and a mis-stated phase predicate would pass both tightness twins. The bounds could also run as monitors on the debug UART~\cite{zilio2023,cho2025}.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
The artifact (models and queries, batch script with retained \texttt{verifyta} outputs and SHA-256 inventory, verdict oracle and checker, line-numbered model-to-source trace, code-level harnesses with retained runs, trace replay with unit tests and JSON report, frozen HIL datasets with inventories) \ifanon is released on acceptance; the firmware is not, so the trace is pinned to a tagged internal revision and source excerpts can be supplied through the chairs.\else is released with this paper; the firmware is not, so the trace is pinned to a tagged internal revision.\fi

\section{Conclusion}
Model checking the composed supervisor, watchdog gate and boot policy of an STM32 flight-software prototype yields worst-case bounds a bench cannot provide and exposes one defect in shipped safety logic with three design consequences. All 368 verdicts across twenty-one configurations equal a pre-declared oracle, the defect is fixed and the fix re-verified, the executed C++ stays within every bound, and replaying frozen hardware traces refutes none of the bounds at the resolution the recorded evidence supports. It shows non-refuting trace consistency with hardware behavior and does not verify the deployed C++ implementation, the RTOS or the HAL.
