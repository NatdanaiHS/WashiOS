## Executive summary

The paper models three interacting safety mechanisms of one STM32 flight-software
prototype — a poll/deadline payload-link supervisor, a task-health-gated IWDG, and an A/B
boot policy — as UPPAAL timed automata, verifies 240 TCTL formulas across 14
configurations against a pre-declared verdict oracle, and replays frozen HIL traces
against the models offline. It reports worst-case bounds shown tight to the millisecond,
one genuine defect (F4, an unconfirmed-boot-limit bypass on the fallback path) and three
design consequences.

The work's real strength is epistemic discipline: a pre-declared oracle, tightness twins
that are *expected to fail*, a falsification floor derived from the evidence rather than
asserted, and an unresolved interval reported as unresolved rather than absorbed. This is
unusually careful for the venue and is the paper's best defence.

The weaknesses that survive are not about rigor but about **verifiability by a reader**:
the models are hand-derived and the firmware is unreleased, so a reviewer must take the
model-to-source correspondence largely on trust. That is the axis on which the paper is
most attackable, and it is also the lecturer's REV-01.

This pass found **two substantive errors** and six clarity/robustness defects, all now
fixed. It also found one hole in the manuscript's own verification gate.

## Summary of contributions

1. Timed-automata models and verified properties for the composed supervisor, watchdog
   gate and boot policy of one concrete prototype, with bounds shown tight.
2. One defect (F4) and three design consequences (F1–F3), all confirmed in source.
3. Model-to-hardware consistency evidence by offline replay of frozen, hash-verified
   traces, with uncertainty quantified from the evidence itself.

## Strengths

### S1: The verdict oracle is pre-declared, and negative results are first-class
Every one of the 240 verdicts is compared against `EXPECTED_VERDICTS.md`, which states in
advance which formulas are *meant* to fail (§IV-A, Table III, the verdict batch). The tightness twins — the
same bound written with `≥`, expected to fail — turn "the bound holds" into "the bound
holds and is attained." This forecloses the standard reviewer objection that a bound was
picked loose enough to pass.

### S2: The measurement uncertainty is derived from the data, not asserted
§III-F does something most systems papers skip: it uses the paired wall-clock/monotonic
stamps in the record to *bound its own timestamp granularity* (15.4 ms max, 4.4 ms
median), then measures the MCU/host clock-rate ratio from the poll counter in the nominal
windows. The 50 ms falsification floor is then justified as >3× the demonstrated
disagreement rather than chosen for convenience.

### S3: The single non-conforming interval is reported, not absorbed
One BAD_CRC recovery interval exceeds its bound by 17 ms and is reported as *unresolved*
(§IV-C). A weaker paper would have widened the floor to swallow it. Keeping it visible is
what makes the other 179 credible.

### S4: The jitter sensitivity sweep pre-empts the obvious attack
The 2 ms jitter bound is assumed, not measured — the paper's softest assumption. Rather
than hiding this, §IV-A re-verifies the boundary configuration at J ∈ {0,1,5} and shows
the undetermined band is a consequence of conservatism (it collapses at J ≤ 1 ms), not a
limitation of the method.

### S5: Honest attribution of discovery
§IV-B states plainly that F1 and F2 were established first by source inspection and only
made precise by the models, and that **only F4 was found by the checker**. Most papers
would claim all four.

## Weaknesses

### Substantive errors found — FIXED

#### W1 — Clock-rate figure contradicted the retained tool output (was: factual error)
§III-F stated the MCU base runs **0.22 %** fast. The retained
`conformance_report.json` computes `host_shorter_by_pct = 0.235`. The derived figure
"1.3 ms at the first-timeout bound" followed from the wrong value (605 × 0.0022 = 1.33);
the correct figure is 605 × 0.00235 = **1.4 ms**. Notably the *same paragraph's* 18 ms
excess calculation used 1.00235 correctly, so the paper was internally inconsistent.

This violated the package's own rule that every conformance number must come from the
retained JSON. **Fixed**: now states 0.235 %, 1.4 ms, and adds that the two 65 s windows
agree to within 0.03 % (they give 0.208 %/0.211 % against the long window's 0.235 %).

#### W2 — F4's severity contradicted property B3 (was: internal inconsistency)
§IV-B F4 concluded "A Pending fallback image therefore gets one attempt **instead of
three**." But `MAX_UNCONFIRMED = 3` with a `bootCount > 3` refusal, so B3 is stated in
Table I as "Pending active slot launched at most **4** times." Three vs four is exactly
the kind of off-by-one a sharp reviewer uses to question whether the authors understand
their own finding. **Fixed**: "instead of the four that the limit of 3 allows (B3)."

### Clarity and robustness defects — FIXED

#### W3 — The W3 bound was not reader-derivable
The properties table (now Table II) gave "deadline + poll + task + J = 952 (link), 1652 (heartbeat)". A reader
naturally reads "task" as the monitored task's check-in period (100 or 500 ms) and cannot
reproduce 952. The term is actually the *watchdog* task's own 50 ms loop delay
(`WD_TASK_MS`). **Fixed**: now "deadline + 100 + 50 + J: 952 (link, deadline 800), 1652
(heartbeat, 1500)", which is arithmetic a reader can check.

#### W4 — The verdict table (now Table III) claims 14 configurations but prints 11 rows
Rows bundle configurations (e.g. "DELAYED 50, 90" is two), and "Q9c" denotes two
reachability formulas — without which the per-row verdict counts don't sum to 19.
**Fixed** in the caption.

#### W5 — The abstract's tightness claim outran its scope
"Each bound is shown tight to the millisecond" reads as a claim about the hardware.
Tightness is a property of the model. **Fixed**: "tight to the millisecond **in the
model**."

#### W6 — F4's practical trigger was unstated
The finding needs the active slot to fail validation *and* the other slot to be Pending.
A reviewer will ask when that arises. **Fixed**: identified as the state left by a staged
but not yet confirmed update.

#### W7 — Symbol `L` used in bounds before being bound to anything
The properties table defines `L = 1`; §III-A describes "once per 1 ms loop" but never connects them.
**Fixed**: `$L=1$` at first use.

### Tooling defect found — FIXED

#### W8 — The manuscript gate could not have caught W1
`verify_manuscript.py` accepted `str(int(round(0.235)))` = `"0"` as a valid rendering, and
`"0" in TEX` is trivially true. The gate reported OK against a wrong number. **Fixed**:
degenerate zero forms are excluded and each candidate must match as a whole numeric token.
Regression-tested — reinstating `0.22` now produces
`FAIL MCU/host rate difference (%) 0.235 missing from manuscript`.

## Methodology assessment

| Criterion | Rating | Assessment |
|---|:--:|---|
| Soundness | 5 | Bounds derived from source constants, verified against a pre-declared oracle, tightness established in both directions. The one arithmetic error found (W1) was in a derived measurement figure, not in a verified bound. |

## Questions for the authors

1. F4 concludes a Pending fallback image gets one launch instead of four. Under what
   deployment sequence does slot B actually end up Pending, given F1 says the application
   never confirms? Is F4 reachable in the shipped provisioning, or only after an OTA
   mechanism that does not yet exist?
2. §IV-A reports the largest state space and query time. On what hardware?
3. The 2 ms jitter bound is assumed. What prevented measuring it directly on the existing
   HIL rig — instrumentation overhead, or scope?
4. The three nominal windows give MCU/host ratios of 1.00208, 1.00211 and 1.00235. Is the
   spread board temperature, host scheduling, or the difference between wall-clock and
   monotonic sampling? The paper quotes only the long window's figure.
5. Would the authors consider stating the F4 fix (reset the count on slot activation) as a
   verified property — i.e. re-running the boot model with the fix applied to show B8 then
   holds?

## Literature positioning

Coverage is good for a 6-page paper and, importantly, the framing is correct throughout:
the paper consistently says "not reported in the cited paper" rather than asserting
absence in the literature. Spacecraft/RTOS (Alencar, Luo, Lin & Wang), communication
supervision (Pan, Johansson), watchdogs (Ahmed & Safar), bootloaders (Cook, Vasudevan,
Yuan) and conformance testing (UPPAAL-TRON) are all represented, each with an explicit
statement of what distinguishes this work.

After REV-02, the UPPAAL-TRON comparison now states what online testing gives that this
does not, and gives an engineering reason for choosing offline replay (no instrumentation
of the flight image, re-checkable without hardware). That closes the strongest "why not
just use TRON?" objection.

The one gap is O3 (VeriBoot), which should not be closed with an unverified citation.
