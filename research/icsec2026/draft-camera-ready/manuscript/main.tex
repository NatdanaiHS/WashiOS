\begin{abstract}
Small-satellite flight software depends on interacting safety mechanisms: poll/deadline supervision of a payload link, a task-health-gated hardware watchdog and a CRC-validated A/B bootloader. Bench tests cannot bound their worst-case behavior, and checking such a composition against the prototype's own hardware is not reported in the cited formal-verification work. We model the three mechanisms of an STM32 flight-software prototype as UPPAAL timed automata, with constants copied from the C++ source, and verify safety, bounded-response and liveness properties in TCTL. All 368 verdicts across 21 configurations match a pre-declared oracle. After confirmed fault onset the first timeout occurs within 603\,ms and OFFLINE within 1607\,ms; recovery completes within 516\,ms; a stalled critical task is detected within 952\,ms and forces a watchdog reset; and the boot policy never launches an unvalidated image. Model checking found one defect, a bypassed unconfirmed-boot limit, now fixed in source. On the target, logic-analyzer timing shows a per-poll wake-up delay of at most 0.57\,ms, below the assumed 2\,ms jitter. Stall and boot-fault injection reproduced the boot defect and its fix and exposed a second defect the models assume away: bootloader and application kept retained state at different addresses. After both fixes every hardware outcome matches the model, the C++ executed on the host stays within every bound, and replay of 90 frozen fault-injection trials refutes none. The guarantees cover the abstracted design, not the deployed C++, the RTOS or the HAL.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
CubeSat-class flight computers rely on a few deterministic safety mechanisms: a polling supervisor that detects a silent, corrupt or late payload; a task-health registry that gates an independent hardware watchdog; and a bootloader that validates an image and otherwise falls back to a second slot or a safe beacon loop. A bench campaign shows only the paths it drove, and host timestamps cannot separate controller timing from serial and OS overhead. An earlier, unpublished hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) campaign on this prototype, whose frozen traces (recorded once and hash-inventoried, never re-collected) we reuse (Section~\ref{sec:conf}), observed the predefined markers in 30/30 trials per fault mode but could not claim controller-internal timing.

This paper asks what the design itself guarantees. We formalize the three mechanisms of an STM32 flight-software prototype (\ifanon name withheld for review; firmware not yet released\else WashiOS\fi) as UPPAAL timed automata~\cite{alur1994,larsen1997,behrmann2004} with constants copied from the source, verify them with \texttt{verifyta}, execute the real C++ against the same properties, and use the frozen HIL traces only as offline consistency evidence.

Contributions: (1)~timed-automata models of the three mechanisms with verified, attained worst-case detection, OFFLINE, recovery and stall-to-reset bounds, the delay-acceptance boundary, and safety and progress properties; (2)~two defects, one found by model checking and one by on-target fault injection, and three design consequences, all confirmed in source (Section~\ref{sec:findings}); (3)~code-level evidence that the real C++ stays within the bounds (Section~\ref{sec:code}); (4)~on-target logic-analyzer measurement showing that the assumed jitter $J{=}2$\,ms is conservative (measured at most 0.57\,ms), with 67 stall injections and 18 boot-fault scenarios (16 flash-page corruptions) covering all three mechanisms (Section~\ref{sec:hw}); (5)~offline replay of frozen traces under an anchor aligned with the model's clocks, which can refute agreement but does not establish conformance.
\textbf{Assurance limits.} We do not claim verification of the C++ implementation, correctness of the RTOS or the vendor hardware abstraction layer (HAL), robustness to environmental or radiation effects, or any reliability estimate (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{RTOS semantics.} UPPAAL has been applied to conformance testing of an ARINC~653 RTOS~\cite{singh2021}, and model checking of the FreeRTOS Cortex-M4 port found errors reproduced on an STM32F429~\cite{lin2024}; both verify the kernel, whereas we verify supervision above an abstracted kernel. UPPAAL has also been applied to satellite communication software~\cite{alencar2014}; link-supervision bounds are not reported in the cited paper.
\textbf{Scaling and properties.} State explosion is met with compositionality and abstraction~\cite{jensen2000}; our three networks stay small (at most 55\,462 states) because their coupling enters each as an input. Pattern catalogs generate observer automata for requirements beyond UPPAAL's fragment~\cite{vogel2023}.
\textbf{Model-to-code assurance.} Integrated formal verification~\cite{ge2018}, verified code generation from timed automata with runtime monitors~\cite{cho2025} and UPPAAL-TRON online testing~\cite{larsen2005} close the model--code gap more strongly than our replay of recorded traces, which shows only that a run did not contradict the model. Model checking has been combined with conformance testing and fault injection for space software~\cite{pontes2014}; fault injection~\cite{arlat1990,batista2019} is the empirical counterpart.
\textbf{The same mechanisms.} CAN fault confinement~\cite{pan2014} and a heartbeat failure detector~\cite{johansson2020} have been verified in UPPAAL, neither with a poll/deadline supervisor and a consecutive-timeout threshold. Watchdogs have been verified by symbolic execution~\cite{ahmed2018}, flown for restart-curable faults~\cite{cutler2022} and used as eager deadlines~\cite{asch2025}, all emphasizing recovery, whereas the gate verified here is fail-safe (F2). Boot code has been verified for memory safety~\cite{cook2020} and secure boot~\cite{vasudevan2024}; slot selection, unconfirmed-boot limits and fallback across resets are not reported in the cited papers.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{System under study}
\label{sec:system}
The controller is a NUCLEO-G431RB (STM32G431RB, 128\,KiB flash, 32\,KiB RAM) running a FreeRTOS application with static allocation and no heap. Every constant below is traced to its source line in the artifact; Table~\ref{tab:trace} shows representative rows.

\textbf{Payload-link supervisor.} A 32-byte CRC-32 framed poll is sent every $P=500$\,ms. A valid response must arrive within $D=100$\,ms of the send instant; the deadline and the next poll are both anchored at that instant. On a timeout the consecutive-timeout counter increments and the link enters OFFLINE when it reaches $N=3$. A valid response resets the counter and sets ONLINE, counting a recovery if the link was OFFLINE. A CRC-corrupted frame increments a counter but leaves the outstanding poll pending; a valid frame that arrives when no poll is pending is a sequence reject. The task services the controller once per $L=1$\,ms loop, receiving frames before evaluating the deadline.

\textbf{Watchdog gate.} Two critical tasks check in periodically: the heartbeat every 500\,ms with a 1500\,ms deadline, and the payload-link task every 100\,ms with an 800\,ms deadline. A watchdog task wakes every 50\,ms and evaluates the registry once 100\,ms have elapsed since the previous evaluation, expiring an entry whose last check-in is older than its deadline; the first expiry records a watchdog-timeout and a safe-fail event in retained memory. The independent watchdog (IWDG), a hardware timer clocked by the internal low-speed oscillator (LSI, $\approx$32\,kHz) that resets the MCU unless refreshed, has a nominal 2.0\,s period (prescaler 16, reload 3999) and is refreshed only while all critical entries are healthy. On this target the safe-fail callback, the handler that logs the failure to retained memory, does not reset the processor; the IWDG does.

\textbf{A/B boot policy.} Each of two flash slots has a state: Empty, Valid, Pending (a staged image the application has not yet confirmed), Confirmed or Bad. On reset the bootloader recovers a CRC-protected fault log and boot metadata from SRAM that survives a warm reset but not power loss, applies a confirmed-boot flag if set, refuses to boot if the active slot is Pending with a boot count above 3 or the fault log holds a check-in failure or watchdog timeout, validates the active slot's state, vector table and CRC-32, falls back to the other slot, and otherwise records a safe-fail and enters an LED beacon loop.



\subsection{Payload-link model}
\label{sec:models}
The network has four templates. \emph{Controller} has clocks for the last poll, the outstanding poll and a received response; its transitions mirror \texttt{preparePoll}, \texttt{acceptResponse} and \texttt{service}, and a received frame is consumed at the next loop pass, before the deadline is evaluated. \emph{Payload} reads its mode when it decodes the request and answers after the round trip below, after a configured delay $d$ (DELAYED), not at all (SILENT) or with a corrupted CRC (BAD\_CRC). \emph{Status} blocks the link task, which then neither polls, evaluates the deadline nor consumes a frame, for at most $S$ once per 5\,s, at any phase. \emph{Host} reproduces the frozen trial protocol (Section~\ref{sec:confmethod}); its transitions are the confirmation events at which the comparison anchors start.

Abstractions: RTOS wake-up and interrupt service routine (ISR) latency are bounded by $J{=}2$\,ms per wake-up; the measured delay is at most 0.57\,ms (Section~\ref{sec:hw}). The round trip is derived from source: the request is decoded 2--9\,ms after the poll stamp (32-byte poll at 115200\,baud, the responder's 1\,ms loop, at most one blocking debug line), the response arrives 2--4\,ms later, and \texttt{HAL\_Delay}$(d)$ lasts $d$ to $d{+}1$\,ms. $S$ entered the model because in the frozen 605\,s observation host stamps show the poll schedule slipping by 10 to 54.5\,ms (median 30.4\,ms, $n{=}241$) at every status report; on the target the write delays no poll by more than 0.40\,ms and the slip comes from the serial reader (Section~\ref{sec:confmethod}), so $S{=}60$ is an over-approximation. The channel is lossless except for the injected corruption; $d<P$. Q1, Q7, Q8, W1, W2 and every boot property do not involve $J$ or $S$, and every bound is a closed form in them (Table~\ref{tab:props}), so a re-measured value moves a bound without invalidating the proof. No $J$ covers blocking or priority inversion, which the code excludes (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).



\subsection{Watchdog-gate model}
Two \emph{Task} instances check in with their periods and may be stalled by an \emph{Env} injector; a \emph{Supervisor} evaluates every 100\,ms with the source's strict-greater expiry test and refreshes the \emph{Iwdg} only when both are healthy; the IWDG resets when its clock reaches a value in $[1882,2170]$\,ms, from the datasheet LSI range of 29.5--34\,kHz~\cite{st2021ds12589}. Scenario~0 never stalls; Scenario~1 stalls one task after warm-up.





\subsection{Boot-policy model}
An untimed network (Fig.~\ref{fig:boot}): \emph{Boot} runs the policy over the retained variables (active slot, slot states, boot count, confirmed flag, retained critical fault, metadata corruption); \emph{App} may stall, setting the retained fault and resetting, reset cleanly, or, in a design variant, set the confirmed flag; \emph{Flash} corrupts an image, stages a Pending image in slot~B, or cold-starts the board, scrubbing retained SRAM. The shipped variant has no confirm edge (F1).

\subsection{Properties}
\label{sec:props}
Table~\ref{tab:props} lists the properties referenced here; the 19, 11 and 14 formulas of the three models are in the artifact. UPPAAL checks three forms of timed computation tree logic (TCTL): \texttt{A[]}\,$\varphi$ ($\varphi$ holds in every reachable state), \texttt{E<>}\,$\varphi$ (some reachable state satisfies $\varphi$) and $\varphi \leadsto \psi$ (leads-to: every $\varphi$-state is eventually followed by a $\psi$-state). A bounded-response property is an \texttt{A[]} formula over a clock started at the confirmation event; its \emph{tightness twin} replaces $>$ by $\ge$ and is expected to fail, which shows that the bound is attained.

\subsection{Consistency-evidence method}
\label{sec:confmethod}
The frozen traces come from a confirmation-gated protocol: a host injects a fault mode over the payload board's debug UART, counts the trial only after the payload confirms the mode, observes the controller console for 4\,s, restores NORMAL, waits for confirmation and observes for 3\,s; both serial channels are logged byte-exact with host timestamps and hash-inventoried. The campaign measured from the command send, which precedes the model's clock start; we measure from the \emph{confirmation anchor}, the host stamp of the payload's confirmation line, to the stamp of the controller's marker. Two uncertainties remain. The command-anchored intervals used a monotonic clock with a 15.6\,ms tick; they disagree with the wall-clock stamps by at most 15.4\,ms. The wall-clock stamps lag the lines they mark: the reader returns 64 bytes or a 50\,ms timeout, so a short line is stamped at the end of a read window, and a long one, such as a status report, ends a window early and shifts every later stamp. Against MCU-side edges with the same reader (Section~\ref{sec:hw}) the lag varies over 0 to 63.8\,ms, and an interval spanning a status report reads 496.5 to 553.0\,ms on the host but 498.1 to 501.2\,ms on the MCU: the slip above. The time bases agree: over 1319 periods the analyzer and host mean poll periods are 500.219 and 500.215\,ms. We declare a \emph{falsification floor} of 50\,ms: the replay refutes only violations larger than the floor and reports smaller excesses as unresolved, for MCU-timed runs to decide.

Every model satisfies \texttt{A[] not deadlock}; progress is stated explicitly (Q11, W5, B9) because the boot environment always offers an enabled edge. Each bound holds strictly and fails with $\ge$, so it is attained; it exceeds a naive derivation by one jitter term per poll period because the next poll is anchored at the actual send instant, which the model caught in our first estimate. Recovery is bounded by 516\,ms, not the 506\,ms of our initial model, because the round trip is now timed from the poll stamp (decode, response, pickup) rather than as a 3--4\,ms response window; against 506\,ms the BAD\_CRC recovery of Section~\ref{sec:conf} appeared 17\,ms over. In the DELAYED 90 band the recovery bound holds but is not attained (worst case 513\,ms); the oracle predicted otherwise and was corrected. The first-timeout bound is $599+2J+S$\,ms; the acceptance edge $d\le 85$\,ms involves neither $J$ nor $S$, whereas the timeout edge $d\ge98+J+S$ moves with both, leaving a band of 86 to 99\,ms, or 86 to 159\,ms with a status report; at $J=5$\,ms, $d=100$\,ms becomes undetermined.

\subsection{Design findings}
\label{sec:findings}
Model checking produced one defect (F4) and made three design consequences precise (F1--F3); on-target fault injection found a second (F5). F1 and F2 were already known from source inspection; F4 was discovered by the checker.

\textbf{F1: the boot-confirmation path is unreachable in flight.} B7 holds in the shipped configuration and fails in the design variant: no application code writes the boot metadata, so a Pending slot can leave that state only through the attempt limit (B3, B8). The prototype is safe because slot~A is provisioned as Confirmed at build time.

\textbf{F2: critical stalls are fail-safe, not fail-operational.} W2 and W6 show that once a critical task expires the IWDG is never refreshed again and a reset follows within one IWDG period. B6 then shows the bootloader, seeing the retained watchdog-timeout event, enters the safe loop until a cold start -- a composed behavior the component tests did not show, and which the hardware exhibited only after F5 was fixed.

\textbf{F3: the effective delay boundary is below the nominal deadline.} Q9 proves acceptance for $d\le 85$\,ms and a timeout with sequence-rejected late frames for $d\ge100$\,ms (160\,ms with a status report); in between both outcomes are reachable. The frozen sweep accepted 90\,ms and rejected 100\,ms in every valid trial on three board pairs, and the executed controller code times out at 90\,ms in 13 of 7\,500 simulated trials, so the band is a property of the code, not of the abstraction.

\textbf{F4: the fallback path bypasses the unconfirmed-boot limit.} B8 fails in both boot configurations, and B8b localizes the violation to the fallback path: the limit check reads the \emph{active} slot, and when that slot fails validation and the other is Pending, the fallback launch inherits the count accumulated by the confirmed slot. Only confirmation resets the count, so a freshly staged Pending slot is launched at counts 0--3 and refused at 4 (B3), but after $k$ warm resets of the confirmed slot the fallback launches it unchecked at count $k$ and refuses it after $4-k$ launches, after one once $k\ge3$ (B8c). The fix resets the attempt budget when the active slot changes; B8 then holds, other boot properties are unchanged, a native regression test fails without the fix, and the exhaustive C++ run (Section~\ref{sec:code}) finds the violation on the pre-fix code only. On the target the fallback runs 3, 2 and 1 times for $k=1,2,3$ before the fix and 4 times after.

\textbf{F5: retained state was not shared.} B3, B6 and F4 assume that the fault log and boot metadata written before a warm reset are read after it. Each image kept its retained objects in its own uninitialized section: the bootloader at \texttt{0x20000018} (metadata) and \texttt{0x20000050} (fault log), the application its fault log at \texttt{0x20000088} and initialized data over both. After an IWDG reset the bootloader therefore found no fault and relaunched the application, and a Pending slot's boot count restarted whenever the application ran; stall injection on the target exposed this. Both images now link one never-initialized region at fixed addresses (metadata, then fault log at +\texttt{0x40}) below all other RAM, and B3, B6 and F4 then hold on the target.

\subsection{Code-level conformance}
\label{sec:code}
We ran the unmodified C++ of the three mechanisms on the host under the model's environment assumptions. The payload-link controller, frame codec and command parser run in a 1\,\textmu s discrete-event simulation of the two task loops, including every blocking UART write; over 287\,500 randomized trials the largest first-timeout, OFFLINE and recovery intervals are 596.7, 1598.6 and 506.4\,ms without a status report and 633.1, 1640.1 and 529.8\,ms with one, all within the bounds, and the delay edges match Q9. The real registry, watchdog and runner, stalled at every 1\,ms phase, detect a link stall within 870.8\,ms and a heartbeat stall within 1586.9\,ms (W3) and never refresh after a failure (W2). An exhaustive search of every retained state reachable under the boot model's environment (324 to 528 states) confirms B1--B4, B6 and B9 and finds B8 violated on the pre-fix code only. The task loops are restated by hand and the payload runs are sampled, so this is conformance evidence, not a refinement proof.

\subsection{On-target measurement and fault injection}
\label{sec:hw}
An instrumented build of the otherwise unchanged control code toggles a pin at every poll send, accepted frame, recovery, loop pass and console write, and the payload marks each confirmed mode change; an 8-channel logic analyzer at 1\,MSa/s gives target time to 1\,\textmu s. An F411 board replaced the unavailable G474 payload of the frozen campaign, which affects only the round trip and the MCU-timed trials. A stall build blocks one critical task 10\,s after boot. Boot faults are injected over the Serial Wire Debug (SWD) port by corrupting a flash page and staging metadata in retained RAM; the bootloader's decision is read at the application's reset vector or after the application has run for 2\,s. Because each poll is scheduled from the actual instant of the previous send (Section~\ref{sec:system}), every period equals $P$ plus the wake-up delay of that poll, so the period excess, at most 0.57\,ms over 1319 periods (Table~\ref{tab:hw}), measures $J$ directly; the assumed $J{=}2$\,ms is 3.5 times the largest value. W3 applies the same $J$ to the 50\,ms watchdog wake-up, which we did not probe; its stall-to-expiry intervals lie 150\,ms (link) and 560\,ms (heartbeat) below the W3 bounds, far more than any plausible $J$. Every MCU-timed interval in Table~\ref{tab:hw} lies within its bound. Expiry to reset takes 1873.4 to 1874.6\,ms, less than the 1882\,ms IWDG minimum, because the IWDG period starts at the refresh of the previous evaluation, at least 100\,ms earlier; that instant was not probed.

\subsection{Consistency with frozen hardware traces}
\label{sec:conf}
The replay accepts every frozen dataset. In all 90 primary trials the predefined detector marker is the model's first observable event for that mode, and no trial recorded a controller restart marker. Both 65\,s nominal windows and the 605\,s observation show zero counter deltas (Q1). In the delay sweep the 3 rows at 50\,ms were accepted (Q9a), the 12 rows at 100 to 250\,ms timed out (Q9b), and the 3 accepted rows at 90\,ms fall in the undetermined band; the two F411 pairs add 18 consistent rows at 100 and 110\,ms and nominal, and 6 rows at 90\,ms, likewise in the band. The 2 BAD\_CRC mechanism traces show the CRC reject before the first timeout (Q7).

Under the confirmation anchor all 180 intervals lie within the bounds that hold in every window, and 179 of the 180 within the tighter bounds for windows without a status report (Table~\ref{tab:desc}). The exception, a BAD\_CRC recovery of 522.8\,ms against 516\,ms, is 6.8\,ms over. Its two stamps come from different serial channels whose lag varies by up to 63.8\,ms, and the 15 MCU-timed recoveries (5 BAD\_CRC) stay within 503.5\,ms (Table~\ref{tab:hw}), so we attribute the excess to the observation path. Because it is smaller than the 50\,ms falsification floor, the replay can neither confirm nor refute it, and we report it as unresolved. In the executed code the host-visible endpoints add up to 4.6\,ms because the recovery marker follows another console line. No interval exceeds a bound by more than the floor, so the replay refutes none; at the command anchor three of the six maxima would exceed them by 7 to 15\,ms.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{Threats to validity and limitations}
The models are hand-derived, and deadlock freedom and progress are model properties. Task-level blocking is excluded by source inspection: the flight code uses no semaphores, queues, mutexes, event groups or task notifications. The trace, code-level runs and replay narrow the model--code gap without proving refinement. Outside the models are the ISRs, UART drivers and HAL, flash programming, the scheduler and the bootloader's CRC-32 and vector-table checks, all treated as atomic environment choices. $J$ and $S$ were measured on one controller board, the round trip and MCU-timed trials with a substitute F411 payload; the channel is assumed lossless; the IWDG bracket comes from the datasheet~\cite{st2021ds12589}, and its period was not measured. Only $J$, the round trip and $S$ depend on the MCU, RTOS and console load, so another target needs them re-measured and the queries re-run. Stalls were injected at one phase, 10\,s after boot, so the narrow stall-to-expiry spread is not the worst case; after the F5 fix, cold starts were emulated by invalidating the retained signatures over SWD, not by removing power. The evidence comes from one controller board and a few payload boards in one configuration and says nothing about device populations, environmental or radiation faults, or qualification.


\subsection{Reproducibility}
The artifact (models, queries, \texttt{verifyta} outputs, verdict oracle, line-numbered model-to-source trace, code-level runs, trace replay with tests, frozen HIL datasets, on-target captures and SWD logs, all with SHA-256 inventories) \ifanon is released on acceptance; the firmware is not, so the trace is pinned to a tagged internal revision and source excerpts can be supplied through the chairs.\else is released at \url{https://github.com/NatdanaiHS/WashiOS}, firmware and scripts on branch \texttt{main}, the rest on \texttt{report}.\fi

\section{Conclusion}
Model checking the composed supervisor, watchdog gate and boot policy of an STM32 prototype yields worst-case bounds a bench cannot provide and exposes a defect in shipped safety logic, now fixed and re-verified; all 368 verdicts across 21 configurations equal the oracle, and the executed C++ stays within every bound. On the target the assumed jitter holds with margin, and fault injection reproduced the boot defect and exposed a second, in retained memory; after its fix every hardware outcome matched the model. The work does not verify the deployed C++, the RTOS or the HAL.
